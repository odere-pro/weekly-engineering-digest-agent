                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  Fine-tuning foundation models is transforming how we apply AI to real-world
                  problems. By adapting pre-trained models to specific domains, we can unlock
                  dramatically better performance on specialized tasks. Today, we’re excited to
                  share how fine-tuning Pixtral-12B on satellite imagery leads to significant
                  improvements over the base model, showcasing the power of domain-specific
                  adaptation.
                </p>
                <h2
                  class="text-h3 font-mistral text-current mb-5 pb-5 border-b border-border-primary not-first:mt-16"
                >
                  LoRA Fine-Tuning can efficiently adapt model weights to specific tasks
                </h2>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  Fine-tuning large language models can be resource-intensive, but techniques like
                  Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) make it far more efficient. LoRA works by injecting
                  small, trainable rank-decomposition matrices into the model&#39;s weights,
                  allowing targeted adaptation without modifying the full model. It enables
                  developers to adapt models to specific tasks, whether it&#39;s learning
                  domain-specific vocabulary, adopting a particular tone, or embedding specialized
                  knowledge, without retraining the entire model.
                </p>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  This method shines when prompt engineering or few-shot examples fall short.
                  Complex prompts can become intricate and hard to maintain, often producing
                  inconsistent results. With LoRA-based fine-tuning, a handful of curated examples
                  can steer the model more reliably, achieving better performance with less
                  overhead.
                </p>
                <h2
                  class="text-h3 font-mistral text-current mb-5 pb-5 border-b border-border-primary not-first:mt-16"
                >
                  The importance of specialized models to satellite imagery
                </h2>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  Satellite imagery is a highly specialized visual domain with critical applications
                  across the global economy. From tracking deforestation and monitoring
                  environmental change to detecting emerging threats, these images power high-stakes
                  decision-making in government, agriculture, defense, and climate science. To
                  extract reliable insights, models must be finely specialized to the unique
                  patterns and semantics of satellite data. This is where fine-tuning Pixtral-12B
                  comes in, bridging the gap between general-purpose vision models and
                  domain-specific expertise.
                </p>
                <h2
                  class="text-h3 font-mistral text-current mb-5 pb-5 border-b border-border-primary not-first:mt-16"
                >
                  Case study: classifying satellite images from the Aerial Image Dataset
                </h2>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  To demonstrate the impact of fine-tuning, we used the Aerial Image Dataset (AID)
                  introduced by Xia et al under a Public Domain license. This benchmark involves
                  classifying satellite images into detailed scene categories. Many of these classes
                  (such as dense residential vs. medium residential; or ambiguous terms like center)
                  are difficult for general vision-language models to handle without domain-specific
                  context. Fine-tuning provides the model with that context, enabling more accurate
                  and nuanced classification.
                </p>

                  especially those with subtle visual distinctions. Additionally, because the
                  language model isn&#39;t explicitly constrained to the label set, it occasionally
                  hallucinated non-existent or invalid class names, highlighting the limitations of
                  purely prompt-based approaches.

                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">Selecting Hyperparameters</p>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  Choosing the right hyperparameters is crucial for successful fine-tuning. Here are
                  some tips:
                </p>
                <ul class="mb-10 pl-5 flex flex-col gap-2 list-disc">
                  <li>
                    <p class="text-body-large text-current">
                      Learning rate: Start with a small learning rate to avoid overshooting the
                      optimal weights.
                    </p>
                  </li>
                  <li>
                    <p class="text-body-large text-current">
                      Batch size: Use a batch size that fits within your computational resources
                      while providing stable gradients.
                    </p>
                  </li>
                  <li>
                    <p class="text-body-large text-current">
                      Epochs: Begin with a single epoch and monitor performance. Additional epochs
                      can be added if necessary but we recommend to keep a close eye on overfitting
                      risk.
                    </p>
                  </li>
                </ul>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  Note: Direct API calls with Mistral fine-tuning API give you more control on the
                  hyperparameters. On LaPlateforme you just need to provide the desired learning
                  rate and number of epochs, the finetuning engine will then compute the optimal
                  batch size based on your dataset size and internal benchmarks of optimal number of
                  tokens per batch.
                </p>
