              <section class="relative w-full">
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  AI agents have proven to be highly capable tools at code generation. Yet, as we
                  push these models to high-stakes domains, ranging from frontier research
                  mathematics to mission-critical software, we encounter a scaling bottleneck: the
                  human review. The time and specialized expertise required to manually verify
                  become the primary impedance of engineering velocity.
                </p>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  We envision a more helpful generation of coding agents to both carry out their
                  tasks and formally prove their implementations against strict specifications.
                  Instead of debugging machine-generated logic, humans dictate what they want.
                  Today, we are taking the first major step toward that vision.
                </p>
                <h2
                  class="text-h3 font-mistral text-current mb-5 pb-5 border-b border-border-primary not-first:mt-16"
                >
                  Introducing Leanstral
                </h2>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  We release Leanstral, the first open-source code agent designed for Lean 4. Lean4
                  is a proof assistant capable of expressing complex mathematical objects such as
                  <a
                    href="https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2020/12/05/liquid-tensor-experiment/"
                    class="underline font-semibold"
                    >perfectoid spaces</a
                  >
                  and software specifications like
                  <a href="https://github.com/AeneasVerif/aeneas" class="underline font-semibold"
                    >properties of Rust fragments</a
                  >. Unlike existing proving systems that act as wrappers around large generalist
                  models or focus on single math problems, Leanstral is designed to be highly
                  efficient (with 6B active parameters) and trained for operating in realistic
                  formal repositories.
                </p>
                <ul class="mb-10 pl-5 flex flex-col gap-2 list-disc">
                  <li>
                    <p class="text-body-large text-current">
                      Open and accessible: We release Leanstral weights under an Apache 2.0 license,
                      in an agent mode within Mistral vibe, and through a free API endpoint. We will
                      also release a tech report detailing our training approach, and a new
                      evaluation suite FLTEval, to move evaluations beyond their focus on
                      competition math.
                    </p>
                  </li>
                  <li>
                    <p class="text-body-large text-current">
                      Efficient and mighty: We use a highly sparse architecture for Leanstral, and
                      optimise it for proof engineering tasks. Leveraging parallel inference with
                      Lean as a perfect verifier, Leanstral is both performant and cost-efficient
                      against existing closed-source competitors.
                    </p>
                  </li>
                  <li>
                    <p class="text-body-large text-current">
                      Upgradable via MCP: Leanstral supports arbitrary MCPs through vibe, and was
                      specifically trained to achieve maximal performance with the frequently used
                      lean-lsp-mcp. 
                    </p>
                  </li>
                </ul>
                <h2
                  class="text-h3 font-mistral text-current mb-5 pb-5 border-b border-border-primary not-first:mt-16"
                >
                  Evaluation
                </h2>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  To reflect usefulness in realistic proof engineering scenarios, we benchmark
                  Leanstral for completing all formal proofs and correctly defining new mathematical
                  concepts in each PR to the FLT project, instead of isolated mathematical problems.
                  We compare Leanstral against leading coding agents (Claude Opus 4.6, Sonnet 4.6,
                  Haiku 4.5) and open-source models (Qwen3.5 397B-A17B, Kimi-K2.5 1T-A32B, GLM5
                  744B-A40B).
                </p>
                <h3 class="text-h4 font-mistral text-current mb-5 not-first:mt-12">
                  Leanstral vs. OSS Models
                </h3>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  Leanstral-120B-A6B demonstrates a significant efficiency advantage over its much
                  larger open-source peers. While models like GLM5-744B-A40B and Kimi-K2.5-1T-32B
                  struggle to scale, capping their FLTEval scores at approximately 16.6 and 20.1
                  respectively, Leanstral outperforms them both with just a single pass.
                </p>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  Even Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, the strongest OSS competitor shown, requires 4 passes to
                  reach a score of 25.4. In contrast, Leanstral achieves a superior score of 26.3
                  with half that investment (pass@2) and continues to scale linearly, reaching 29.3
                  at the same cost level.
                </p>

                <h3 class="text-h4 font-mistral text-current mb-5 not-first:mt-12">
                  Leanstral vs. Claude Family
                </h3>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  Leanstral serves as a high-value alternative to the Claude suite, offering
                  competitive performance at a fraction of the price: Leanstral pass@2 reaches a
                  score of 26.3, beating Sonnet by 2.6 points, while costing only $36 to run,
                  compared to Sonnet’s $549. At pass@16, Leanstral reaches a score of 31.9,
                  comfortably beating Sonnet by 8 points. While Claude Opus 4.6 remains the leader
                  in quality, it carries a staggering cost of $1,650, 92 times higher than running
                  Leanstral.
                </p>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  In our benchmarking, we used Mistral Vibe as the scaffold with no modifications
                  specifically for the evaluation.

                >
                  Case studies
                </h2>
                <h3 class="text-h4 font-mistral text-current mb-5 not-first:mt-12">
                  Answering stackexchange posts about changes in newest Lean version
                </h3>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  When breaking changes hit a new Lean release, migrating code can be a massive
                  headache. We fed Leanstral
                  <a
                    href="https://proofassistants.stackexchange.com/questions/6471/a-strange-issue-with-a-type-alias-in-lean4"
                    target="_blank"
                    rel="noopener noreferrer"
                    class="underline font-semibold"
                    >a real-world question from the Proof Assistants Stack Exchange</a
                  >
                  about a script that mysteriously stopped compiling in Lean 4.29.0-rc6 (which we
                  did not train with due to its recency). The culprit was a rewrite (<code
                    class="bg-surface-brand-secondary border border-border-primary rounded-sm px-2 py-px text-text-brand-8 dark:text-text-brand-7 text-base"
                    >rw</code
                  >) tactic that suddenly failed to match patterns involving a simple type alias,
                  initially written as
                  <code
                    class="bg-surface-brand-secondary border border-border-primary rounded-sm px-2 py-px text-text-brand-8 dark:text-text-brand-7 text-base"
                    >def T2 := List Bool</code
                  >.
                </p>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  Instead of taking a stab in the dark, Leanstral rolled up its sleeves. It
                  successfully built test code to recreate the failing environment and diagnosed the
                  underlying issue with definitional equality. The model correctly identified that
                  because def creates a rigid definition requiring explicit unfolding, it was
                  actively blocking the rw tactic from seeing the underlying structure it needed to
                  match.
                </p>
                <p class="text-body-large text-text-primary mb-10">
                  The fix it proposed was simple: just swap
                  <code
                    class="bg-surface-brand-secondary border border-border-primary rounded-sm px-2 py-px text-text-brand-8 dark:text-text-brand-7 text-base"
                    >def</code
                  >
                  for
                  <code
                    class="bg-surface-brand-secondary border border-border-primary rounded-sm px-2 py-px text-text-brand-8 dark:text-text-brand-7 text-base"
                    >abbrev</code
                  >. Because
                  <code
                    class="bg-surface-brand-secondary border border-border-primary rounded-sm px-2 py-px text-text-brand-8 dark:text-text-brand-7 text-base"
                    >abbrev</code
                  >
                  creates a transparent alias that is immediately definitionally equal to the
                  original type, the
                  <code
                    class="bg-surface-brand-secondary border border-border-primary rounded-sm px-2 py-px text-text-brand-8 dark:text-text-brand-7 text-base"
                    >rw</code
                  >
                  tactic could once again perfectly match the pattern
                  <code
                    class="bg-surface-brand-secondary border border-border-primary rounded-sm px-2 py-px text-text-brand-8 dark:text-text-brand-7 text-base"
                    >(L2 n).length</code
                  >
                  in the proof. Leanstral completes the job and explains the rationale to the user
                  perfectly. 
                </p>
