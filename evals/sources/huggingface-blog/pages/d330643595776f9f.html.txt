<p><!--[--><!--[0--><div class="absolute -left-12 bottom-0 top-0 z-30 not-prose hidden lg:block"><div class="sticky top-4 flex"><div class="h-7 pt-[0.175rem]"><span class="peer" tabindex="0"><button class="select-none text-gray-400 hover:cursor-pointer hover:text-gray-800 dark:text-gray-500 dark:hover:text-gray-400"> <!----></button></span> <div class="invisible w-0 -translate-x-8 -translate-y-6 overflow-hidden rounded-xl border bg-white transition-transform hover:visible hover:w-52 hover:translate-x-0 peer-focus-within:visible peer-focus-within:w-52 peer-focus-within:translate-x-0"> </div></div></div></div><!--]--><!--]-->Introduced last spring, the <a href="https://github.com/generative-computing/granite-switch" rel="nofollow">Granite Switch</a> project enables a modular approach to model development. Now <strong>Granite Switch 4.1</strong> models, armed with a Decision adapter and the fastest ASR model on the OpenASR leaderboard, can move efficiently between natural-language responses, general and specialized decisions, and speech-to-text tasks—all from one hosted endpoint.</p>
<p><a href="https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/662836b742709fe826510597/LHBbw-MVVQgnYcpDK_DWC.png" rel="nofollow"><img src="https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/662836b742709fe826510597/LHBbw-MVVQgnYcpDK_DWC.png" alt="granite_switch_adapter_swap"></a></p>
<p>Specifically, <a href="https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-switch-4.1-8b">Granite-Switch-4.1-8B</a> and <a href="https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-switch-4.1-30b">-30B</a> models are configured to include:</p>
<ol>
<li>A new <a href="https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granitelib-core-r1.0/blob/main/decision/README.md">Decision aLoRA</a> for general-purpose decision-making (e.g. v1/systemone),</li>
<li>A specialized <a href="https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granitelib-guardian-r1.0/blob/main/guardian-core/README.md">Guardian aLoRA</a> that, on average, outperforms Jev 1.13, Microsoft-Decision-1, GPT-Luna-Decisions, and Clef-Flash on safety-related decision tasks, and</li>
<li><a href="https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-speech-5.0-470m-turboctc">Granite-Speech-5.0-Turbo-CTC</a>, the fastest ASR model on the <a href="https://huggingface.co/spaces/hf-audio/open_asr_leaderboard">OpenASR Leaderboard</a>, embedded for use in transcription tasks.</li>
</ol>
<p>All three of these technologies (along with the underlying base model) are composed into one model, taking up approximately the same amount of VRAM on a GPU as the base model would by itself. That means more capabilities with fewer GPUs.</p>
<p>And because the Switch adapters were built as cache-efficient <a href="https://research.ibm.com/blog/inference-friendly-aloras-lora" rel="nofollow">Activated LoRAs</a>, the KV cache created from a base model generation can be reused for any subsequent adapter-based decisions. Combined with our team's <a href="https://github.com/generative-computing/granite-switch/blob/main/docs/SWITCH_KERNELS.md" rel="nofollow">recent vLLM contributions</a> to speed up low rank adapter inferencing, as well as a co-located ASR encoder, Granite Switch 4.1 models can <em>switch</em> between a chat request and a decision request and a spoken request, with blazing fast speeds.</p>
<p><em>Check out our <a href="https://huggingface.co/spaces/ibm-granite/speech-to-decisions">Speech-to-Decisions HF space</a> where granite-switch-4.1-8b transcribes audio, generates summaries, screens for harmful content, and tests 15 other user-defined flags—all running on a single endpoint in real time.</em></p>

<h2 class="relative group flex items-baseline">
	<a id="granite-libraries-the-og-decision-models" class="block pr-1.5 text-lg md:absolute md:p-1.5 md:opacity-0 md:group-hover:opacity-100 md:right-full" href="#granite-libraries-the-og-decision-models" rel="nofollow">
		<span class="header-link"> </span>
	</a>
	<span>
		Granite Libraries: The OG Decision Models
	</span>
</h2>
<p>Decision models aren’t new. Our previous release of <a href="https://huggingface.co/collections/ibm-granite/granite-libraries">Granite Libraries</a> features a repository of specialized “decision model” adapters and other specialized task adapters that can be composed into custom Granite Switch models. And while Jev and other closed decision models focus on generality, we’ve found that many of our specialized granitelib adapters can outperform generalists on specialized classification tasks.</p>
<p><em><a href="https://huggingface.co/spaces/ibm-granite/granitelib-evals">Compare granitelib adapters</a>  with generalist decision models when evaluated on specialized classification tasks.</em></p>
<iframe src="https://ibm-granite-granitelib-evals.static.hf.space" frameborder="0" width="100%" height="725" sandbox="allow-scripts" referrerpolicy="same-origin"></iframe>

<p>Because adapters share the base model's KV cache, adding specialists alongside a generalist decision adapter costs almost nothing. You get broad coverage from the generalist and higher accuracy from specialists on the tasks you care about.</p>
<p><em>In a “Race” between Granite-Switch-4.1-30B (frozen base Granite + 4 specialized aLoRAs) and Jev-27B (frozen base Qwen model + 1 generic decision LoRA), Granite-Switch-4.1-30B was able to complete a real world, 5-step RAG workflow much faster than its competitor.</em></p>
<p><video src="https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/662836b742709fe826510597/dbYUTX2G9CrHjHp5knXkr.mp4" controls="" class="max-w-full!"></video></p>

<h2 class="relative group flex items-baseline">
	<a id="compose-and-extend-your-own-granite-switch-model" class="block pr-1.5 text-lg md:absolute md:p-1.5 md:opacity-0 md:group-hover:opacity-100 md:right-full" href="#compose-and-extend-your-own-granite-switch-model" rel="nofollow">
		<span class="header-link"> </span>
	</a>
	<span>
		Compose and Extend Your Own Granite Switch Model
	</span>
</h2>
<p>Granite-Switch-4.1-8B and -30B are released with “batteries included”. They are Granite Switch models that we compiled with a predefined set of helpful adapters. But you don’t need to use our version. Anyone can <a href="https://generative-computing.github.io/granite-switch/adapter_catalog.html" rel="nofollow">compose their own Granite Switch model</a>, mixing and matching adapters from Granite Libraries to best meet their needs.</p>
<p>Moving forward, we see tremendous opportunity for the community to compose and extend Granite Switch models with custom and third party adapters. We plan to release an Adapter SDK for training adapters in <a href="https://docs.mellea.ai/advanced/lora-and-alora-adapters" rel="nofollow">Mellea</a>, along side other tutorials and Granite Switch support. These adapters could be specialized classification adapters to augment decision-based workflows or adapters for specialized language tasks. You could even use custom adapters to teach a 3B parameter version of Granite to play the classic game Doom.</p>
<p><em>Similarly to Jev, our custom 3B parameter Granite Switch model made split second decisions to navigate the Doom game, but was able to do so at <strong>60 - 100 decisions / second,</strong> compared to Jev’s reported 10 decisions / second.</em><sup><a href="#footnote-1" rel="nofollow">1</a></sup> <em>Unlike Jev, this Granite Switch model can also narrate and respond to your questions while it is playing.</em></p>
<p><video src="https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/662836b742709fe826510597/e4iQFxtaMARGMfy7xVrUm.qt" controls="" class="max-w-full!"></video></p>
We look forward to releasing more details and data on Granite Switch in the upcoming weeks, but until then, we encourage you to try out our latest Switch models and these other helpful resources for getting started.

<h2 class="relative group flex items-baseline">
	<a id="accessible-from-anywhere" class="block pr-1.5 text-lg md:absolute md:p-1.5 md:opacity-0 md:group-hover:opacity-100 md:right-full" href="#accessible-from-anywhere" rel="nofollow">
		<span class="header-link"> </span>
	</a>
	<span>
		Accessible from anywhere
	</span>
</h2>
<p>Coming in 8b and 30b sizes, Granite Switch 4.1 can fit wherever your workload runs. The model runs in vllm with a plug-in (see Granite Switch repo below), and has native inference support in <code>llama.cpp</code> and downstream platforms which allows the model to seamlessly swap in for <code>/v1/systemone</code> API calls anywhere they're used, while also servicing traditional chat calls, all with a shared prefix cache.</p>
