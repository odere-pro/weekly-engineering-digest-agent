                Security Bug Bounty Program Paused Due to Loss of Funding
              </div>
            </div>
            <p>
              The Node.js project&#x27;s security bug bounty program is being paused due to the
              discontinuation of its external funding source.
            </p>
            <h2 id="background"><a href="#background">Background</a></h2>
            <p>
              Since 2016, the Node.js project has participated in the
              <a href="https://www.hackerone.com/internet-bug-bounty">Internet Bug Bounty (IBB)</a>
              program through HackerOne, offering monetary rewards to security researchers who
              responsibly disclosed vulnerabilities in Node.js. The program was a meaningful part of
              our security ecosystem, and we&#x27;re grateful to the researchers who participated.
            </p>
            <h2 id="why"><a href="#why">Why</a></h2>
            <p>
              The Internet Bug Bounty (IBB) program, which supported bounty rewards for Node.js
              through a pooled donation-funded initiative, has been paused. You can read more about
              the pause <a href="https://hackerone.com/ibb?type=team">here</a>. This decision was
              not made by the Node.js project.
            </p>
            <p>
              As a volunteer-driven open-source project, Node.js does not have an independent budget
              to sustain a bounty program on its own. Without external support, we are not able to
              offer monetary rewards for vulnerability reports at this time.
            </p>
            <h2 id="what-this-means"><a href="#what-this-means">What This Means</a></h2>
            <ul>
              <li>
                <strong>Security reporting remains unchanged.</strong> We still accept and triage
                vulnerability reports through <a href="https://hackerone.com/nodejs">HackerOne</a>.
                If you discover a security issue, please continue to report it responsibly.
              </li>
              <li>
                <strong>No monetary rewards.</strong> Reports will no longer be eligible for bounty
                payouts.
              </li>
              <li>
                <strong>Same commitment to security.</strong> The Node.js Security Team continues to
                treat security with the highest priority. Our disclosure policy, response times, and
                release process remain the same.
              </li>
            </ul>
            <h2 id="a-thank-you-to-researchers">
              <a href="#a-thank-you-to-researchers">A Thank You to Researchers</a>
            </h2>
            <p>
              We want to sincerely thank every researcher who has reported vulnerabilities through
              the bounty program over the years. Your contributions have made Node.js safer for
              millions of users. We hope you will continue to report security issues even without
              financial incentives — responsible disclosure is critical to the health of the
              open-source ecosystem.
            </p>
            <h2 id="looking-ahead"><a href="#looking-ahead">Looking Ahead</a></h2>
            <p>
              We will re-evaluate resuming the bounty program if dedicated funding becomes available
              again. If your organization depends on Node.js and is interested in sponsoring a bug
              bounty program, please reach out through the
              <a href="https://openjsf.org/">OpenJS Foundation</a>.
            </p>
            <p>
              For questions or to report a vulnerability, see our
              <a href="/en/about/security-reporting">security reporting page</a>.
            </p>
