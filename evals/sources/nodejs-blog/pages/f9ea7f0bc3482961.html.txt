                New HackerOne Signal Requirement for Vulnerability Reports
              </div>
            </div>
            <p>
              <strong>UPDATE 2026-02-19</strong>: New researchers without signal can no longer
              submit reports through HackerOne. If you are a new researcher and would like to report
              a potential vulnerability, please reach out to the
              <a href="https://github.com/nodejs/node?tab=readme-ov-file#security-release-stewards"
                >Node.js security release stewards</a
              >
              through the <a href="https://slack-invite.openjsf.org/">OpenJS Foundation Slack</a>.
            </p>
            <hr />
            <p>
              We have updated our <a href="https://hackerone.com/nodejs">HackerOne program</a> to
              require a <strong>Signal of 1.0 or higher</strong> to submit vulnerability reports to
              the Node.js project.
            </p>
            <h2 id="why-this-change"><a href="#why-this-change">Why This Change</a></h2>
            <p>
              The Node.js security team has experienced a significant increase in low-quality
              reports. This trend has been increasing over the years, and over the holidays it
              crossed the threshold that we can actually handle. Between December 15th and January
              15th, we received over 30 reports. Triaging these reports consumes time and energy
              that could be spent on legitimate security work.
            </p>
            <p>
              By requiring a minimum Signal score, we ensure that reporters have a proven track
              record of submitting valid security reports, while still allowing newer researchers to
              participate with a limited number of submissions.
            </p>
            <h2 id="what-this-means-for-you">
              <a href="#what-this-means-for-you">What This Means for You</a>
            </h2>
            <ul>
              <li>
                <strong
                  >New researchers or researchers with
                  <a href="https://docs.hackerone.com/en/articles/8369891-signal-impact">signal</a>
                  &gt;= 1.0</strong
                >: You can continue reporting vulnerabilities through HackerOne as usual
              </li>
              <li>
                <strong>Those below the threshold</strong>: You can still reach the security team
                through the
                <a href="https://slack-invite.openjsf.org/">OpenJS Foundation Slack</a> (channel:
                <code>#nodejs-security-wg</code> for help). Contact us directly using direct
                messages there to discuss potential vulnerabilities. You can find the users listed
                in
                <a href="https://github.com/nodejs/node#security-release-stewards"
                  >security release stewards</a
                >.
              </li>
            </ul>
            <h2 id="about-hackerone-signal">
              <a href="#about-hackerone-signal">About HackerOne Signal</a>
            </h2>
            <p>
              <a href="https://docs.hackerone.com/en/articles/8369891-signal-impact">Signal</a> is
              HackerOne&#x27;s reputation metric that reflects the quality of a researcher&#x27;s
              past submissions. A higher Signal indicates a history of valid, impactful reports.
              This requirement helps us prioritize reports from researchers with demonstrated
              expertise while reducing the burden of triaging invalid submissions.
            </p>
            <p>
              We appreciate the security community&#x27;s understanding and continued collaboration
              in keeping Node.js secure.
            </p>
